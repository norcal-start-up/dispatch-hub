# Job Prioritization
Platform: ServiceTitan
Status: Active

## When This Is Used

When you have more work than technicians. Higher priorities can bump lower ones.

## Priority 1: Emergency service

An immediate system failure or safety risk that needs a same-day response.

### Examples

- No cooling or heating in extreme weather
- Data center or server room cooling failure
- Refrigeration loss that affects inventory
- A system creating a safety hazard
- A critical tenant or hospital environment failure

### Scheduling rule

- If it's a true emergency, dispatch immediately.
- Typical response: within 2 to 4 hours, depending on the contract.
- Technicians may be pulled from lower-priority work, with the Field Supervisor's OK.

## Priority 2: Contracted service and critical customers

Customers whose service agreements require a priority response.

### Examples

- National accounts
- Facilities with uptime requirements
- PM agreement customers reporting failures

### Scheduling rule

- Comes before non-contract work.
- Typical response: same day or next day.
- Technicians may be reassigned if needed.

## Priority 3: Quoted repair work

Approved repairs that were diagnosed and quoted earlier.

### Examples

- Major component repairs
- Approved corrective repairs
- Coil and compressor replacements

### Scheduling rule

- Scheduled within a reasonable timeframe, usually days.
- Can slip briefly for emergencies, but shouldn't stall.

## Priority 4: Time and material service calls

Diagnostic or repair calls billed by the hour without a quote.

### Examples

- Diagnostics
- Minor repairs
- Thermostat issues
- A unit that isn't cooling well

### Scheduling rule

- Usually handled in the daily dispatch rotation.
- Scheduled around emergencies and approved work.
- May be rescheduled if higher-priority work comes in.

## Priority 5: Preventive maintenance

Scheduled routine maintenance.

### Examples

- Coil cleaning
- Filter changes
- Seasonal inspections

### Scheduling rule

- Lowest operational priority.
- Often used to fill gaps in the schedule.
- Can move if service demand spikes.
- Aim to finish within three months of the PM schedule.
